Job search with soft range matching

The salary and experience ranges on each posting become vectors. One dot product per posting then scores keyword match, pay fit and years fit together, and each range fit stays within 0.001 of its exact value. Anything that ranks by inner product, a vector database included, can run the same query.

      How it works
      
  
      Pay fit is the share of the posted band above your floor, minimum ÷ (1 + flexibility), once both edges carry Gaussian slack: yours, and the employer's stretch. Experience fit is the chance your years land in the required range after the employer's stretch moves each edge out, under the same kind of slack. Both are integrals of a smoothed indicator, and Parseval's identity turns each into a sum over Fourier harmonics. The encoder writes one side's half of every harmonic into the posting vector and the other half into the query. The two slacks multiply as separate Gaussian factors, so each side carries its own.
      
  
      fit(A, B, σ) = (1/|A|) ∫_A (1_B ∗ N(0, σ²))(x) dx,     σ² = σ_seeker² + σ_employer²
vector(I, s) = (1/√P)·[ L,  √2·L·sinc(ω_k·h)·e^(−s²ω_k²/2)·cos(ω_k·m),  …sin(ω_k·m) ]  k = 1..K,  ω_k = 2πk/P
               m, h: midpoint and half-width of I;  L = 1 on the measured side, |I| on the other
               P = D + 2E + 2W: axis length D plus the open-end reach E and the guard gap W at both ends
|query · posting − fit| ≤ Σ_(k>K) (2/πk)·e^(−2π²σ_min²k²/P²) + Φ̄(E/σ_max) + 2·Φ̄(2W/σ_max) ≤ ε
      
  
      K is the smallest count that meets ε. At ε = 0.001 pay uses 133 numbers and experience 113. The frequencies are fixed, so the bound is deterministic and holds for every pair. A flat scan whose error stays within Δ can prove its top-k exact: it re-scores until the k-th exact score beats the best unseen approximate score plus Δ, the multi-step k-NN rule of Seidl and Kriegel (1998).
